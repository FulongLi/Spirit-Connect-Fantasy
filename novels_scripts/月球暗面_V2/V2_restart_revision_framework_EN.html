The Dark Side of the Moon V2 Restart Revision Framework

Date: 2026-06-10 Current principle: V2 uses V1 as the source manuscript. The previous mistaken rewrite direction is no longer the basis. Chapters 1 and 2 are voice anchors and should not be substantially rewritten.

1. What This Restart Corrects

The earlier V2 direction drifted toward “writing a cleaner new version of the novel.” That is not the current goal.

The correct V2 should:

  • Preserve the V1 storyline, chapter order, and first-person narrative temperament.
  • Protect Chapter 1, “Journey to the Dark Side,” and Chapter 2, “New Century,” from content-level rewriting.
  • Revise later chapters only where pacing, motivation, information flow, or character logic needs help.
  • Use the novel-writing skill to judge scene function, character anchoring, access to information, and style fidelity.
  • Use Markdown as the collaboration/editing format and HTML as the reading format.

The earlier mistaken V2 is not the current source. The current 月球暗面_V2 folder has been reset from V1.

2. Core V2 Goal

V2 should not make the manuscript generically polished. It should help the fate line already present in V1 become more natural and inevitable.

The main line remains:

During his second digital correction, Jim enters the dark side of the Moon and discovers that it is not a normal dream or virtual anomaly, but an entry point into Terasha records and a higher structure of consciousness. Anna and Charlie are trapped. Dr. Lune, Mr. Spire, and older-generation researchers each hold incomplete pieces of the truth. Jim eventually chooses to die on the Moon, entering the real lunar system to rescue his friends and open the first gate of the series.

The revision should strengthen:

  • Scene pressure behind each revelation.
  • Decisions that grow out of what characters have already experienced.
  • A sense of inevitability created by accumulated choices, not authorial force.
  • Reader attachment to danger, doubt, and relationships before large-scale exposition.

3. Protection Rules for Chapters 1 and 2

Chapter 1: Journey to the Dark Side

Protection level: highest.

This chapter is the most important voice sample in the book. Its value is not only plot, but the state of narration: half dream, half scientific expedition; half fear, half curiosity.

It contains:

  • Jim observing even while frightened.
  • Vast images entering through bodily sensations: black water, the stone gate, desert, rice field, time slices, black and white spaces, and the blue-black sphere.
  • Uncertainty, speculation, sudden humor, and self-doubt.
  • Worldbuilding that appears as fragments Jim touches, not facts explained to him.

Allowed in V2:

  • Clear typo, punctuation, and paragraphing fixes.
  • Light splitting of overly long paragraphs.
  • Minimal name or term alignment if required by the series bible.

Not allowed in V2:

  • Changing the chapter into third person or omniscient narration.
  • Turning the dreamlike exploration into a clean mission sequence.
  • Removing Jim’s hesitation, confusion, bodily reactions, or digressive thoughts.
  • Explaining Elara, Terasha, the Time Well, or the lunar system too early.

Chapter 2: New Century

Protection level: highest.

This chapter is the voice sample for how the real world enters the book. Its function is not to present a perfect science-fiction handbook, but to let Jim explain his era in his own way:

  • The dual-born model.
  • Digital correction.
  • Spirit Connect.
  • Another World.
  • The age-eighteen accident.
  • The first direct threat from Digital Jim.

Allowed in V2:

  • Name unification.
  • Very small ordering adjustments if readers become temporarily lost.
  • Punctuation, typo, and long-paragraph cleanup.

Not allowed in V2:

  • Removing Jim’s explanatory voice.
  • Replacing social worldbuilding with encyclopedia entries.
  • Flattening Digital Jim’s ambiguity too early.
  • Making Dr. Lune sound as if he already knows the entire cosmic truth.

6. Key Problems and Revision Directions

1. Earth civilization history feels too broad

Current issue: Chapters 3 and 5 present life origins, human civilization, myth, Roswell, the Black Tide, consciousness, and souls in long consecutive passages. This can read like a lecture.

Revision direction:

  • Break the history into triggers: anomalous images in lunar records, experts disagreeing, old archives revealing partial truth, and Lune or Spire concealing another part.
  • Make each historical passage answer an immediate question: why can’t Anna and Charlie return, why is the Moon essential, why is Jim the key, why can’t Spirit Connect go public?
  • Keep the core idea that myth is an early human description of higher technology, but reduce abstract explanation.

2. The trio’s entry into Another World needs more force

Current issue: Chapter 4 contains the trio’s first game experience, but later shifts too easily into project explanation.

Revision direction:

  • Make the game section reveal character function: Jim intervenes when he does not have to, Anna reads rules, Charlie gives the ordinary human reaction and emotional temperature.
  • The hidden quest should not be only an adventure; it should foreshadow their later fates.
  • Compress technical explanation into character interaction, mission feedback, or internal document fragments.

3. Jim going to the Moon feels too quick

Current issue: Chapters 6 and 7 point in the right direction, but the movement from discovery to lunar death needs more psychological, ethical, and practical weight.

Revision direction:

  • Let Jim try other paths first: repeated calls, Lune’s checks, digital routes, older researchers’ theories.
  • Make “death on the Moon” emerge from Shawn’s theory, Elara’s hints, Jim’s lunar experience, and Anna/Charlie’s crisis together.
  • Dr. Lune should not only assist; he should resist, witness, and finally let go.
  • Jim’s decision should hold fear, responsibility, impulse, and concrete memories of his friends.

4. Balance between cosmology and character fate

Current issue: The worldbuilding is imaginative, but some passages temporarily remove the characters from the center.

Revision direction:

  • After every worldbuilding passage, ask: whose decision has changed because of this?
  • Every character who speaks exposition should bring a position, fear, or interest.
  • Spire should not be only an explainer; he should carry the danger of failed digital immortality and obsession with migration.
  • Lune should not be only a scientist; he is Jim’s last human connection on the real-world side.

Status: complete.

Pass 1: Minimal cleanup

Scope:

  • Unify names.
  • Fix clear typos, punctuation, and repeated speech-input errors.
  • Lightly split overly long paragraphs.

Principle:

  • Chapters 1 and 2 receive only the minimum needed touch.
  • No structural rewriting yet.

Pass 2: Chapter cards

Create chapter cards for Chapters 3 through 7. Each card should state:

  • What problem this chapter solves.
  • Who changes in this chapter.
  • What information must be revealed.
  • What information must be delayed.
  • Which passages carry the author’s original voice and must be protected.

Pass 3: Prose revision

Revise one chapter at a time, without cross-chapter rewriting.

Recommended order:

1. Chapter 3, “Lunar Records”: rebuild civilization-history information flow.

2. Chapter 4, “Another World”: rebuild the game section and trio function.

3. Chapter 5, “Half a Century”: ground the Black Tide and old-world testimony.

4. Chapter 6, “Endless Waiting”: make Jim’s choice to go to the Moon feel inevitable.

5. Chapter 7, “Approaching the Truth”: preserve the destination and strengthen the final movement.

Pass 4: HTML sync

After each chapter revision, generate:

  • Markdown revision draft.
  • HTML reading draft.
  • Index or revision record updates if needed.

Pass 5: User confirmation

After each chapter, the user reads the HTML version; Codex continues editing the Markdown version based on feedback.
