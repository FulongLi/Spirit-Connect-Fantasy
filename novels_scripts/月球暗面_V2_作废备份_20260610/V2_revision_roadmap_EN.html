Moon's Dark Side V2 Revision Roadmap

Date: 2026-06-06

1. Overall Goal

V2 should not throw away V1. Its goal is to make the story feel driven by character, rules, and planted causes rather than by the author's hand.

Core direction:

Everything should feel fated, not forced.

V2 should focus on three major improvements:

1. The Earth civilization history section is too broad and should become a focused chain of discovery.

2. The *Another World* game section should become the emotional root of the main trio.

3. Jim's journey to the Moon and physical death currently feel rushed and should become the thematic ending the whole book has been moving toward.

2. What Can Stay

Moon-Dark Imagery

The stone gate, ancient battlefield, desert, rice fields, time slices, white realm, blue-black sphere, black realm, and space-time well are among the strongest parts of V1.

The route should remain, but the explanation density should be reduced so the experience feels like contact with something not fully knowable.

The Main Trio's Core Traits

Jim: the unnecessary intervener.

Anna: the reader of rules.

Charlie: afraid, but does not retreat when it matters.

These traits are strong. V2 should show them through events rather than explain them.

Elara's Mystery

The mysterious little girl / Elara should remain gentle but high-permission, intimate but not fully trustworthy.

Her identity should not be explained too early. Each rescue should carry a limit or cost.

Digital Jim

Digital Jim should not be a simple villain. He is another possible Jim: colder, closer to truth, and more dangerous.

V2 should plant earlier signs that he has not vanished, but is waiting deeper inside the system.

Ending Direction

Jim choosing to reach the real Moon's dark side to save Anna, Charlie, and the others is the right ending direction.

The execution needs expansion so it feels like the natural destination of the whole book.

3. What Needs Major Improvement

Earth Civilization History Should Not Read Like A Report

In V1, the civilization history section contains many ideas but reads too much like a meeting lecture. V2 should turn it into a discovery chain:

1. Jim brings back unclear fragments.

2. Jared finds corresponding mythic structures.

3. Professor Cross and Dr. Shawn offer different interpretations from data.

4. Spirit Connect discovers that certain records cannot be fabricated by the system.

5. Conclusions emerge gradually rather than all at once.

The section should not cover every ancient civilization equally. It should choose three or four strong cases and give each a clear function.

Recommended focus:

  • Sumer: memory of humans being modified or placed.
  • China: hun/po, sky repair, Nuwa/Fuxi as metaphors for repair and teaching.
  • Egypt: Ka, Ba, Akh as layers of consciousness.
  • Maya: repeated failed creations as experimental iterations.

The Game Section Should Root The Trio

*Another World* should not be a side memory. It should be where the system first records the trio's fate pattern.

The game section should:

1. Show why Jim intervenes.

2. Show why Anna reads rules.

3. Show why Charlie fears danger but still stays.

The moment Jim blocks the soldier should be kept and expanded. It is a rehearsal for his final fate.

The Moon Journey Should Become A Fate Convergence

The late V1 direction is right, but too fast. V2 should split the Moon arc into three layers:

1. Before the decision: Jim knows the cost but has not fully understood death.

2. Before launch: he faces the emotional pressure of Anna, Charlie, Dr. Lune, and Mr. Spire.

3. After arrival: the Moon is no longer a mission site, but the endpoint of Jim's lifelong tendency to intervene.

The key is not aerospace technology, but:

Jim finally enters a place where no one asked him to be responsible, yet only he can be responsible.

Viewpoint Needs Rules

Recommended V2 viewpoint rules:

  • Jim's direct experiences and interiority: first person.
  • Spirit Connect, meetings, Spire, and Lune on the reality side: third person.
  • Author's preface and Jim's foreword: meta-narrative can remain.

Avoid switching between "I" and "Jim" inside the same small section.

Names And Terms Must Be Unified

V2 has already started the mechanical name update:

  • Jim Dickens → Jim Vale
  • Charlie Filler → Charlie Hale
  • Mr. Spelicon → Mr. Spire
  • Professor Bao → Professor Cross
  • Dr. Xiao → Dr. Shawn
  • Charles Wheeler → Arthur Wheeler

Later passes should also unify terms such as digital correction, Moon's dark side, Terasha Record, subject-core, and non-Earth death zone.

4. Suggested V2 Work Order

Pass 1: Structure And Names

Create the V2 folder, unify character names, create the roadmap, and rebuild HTML reading files.

Pass 2: Reorder And Rewrite The First Four Chapters

Let readers know the trio before entering the Moon-Dark experience.

Suggested order:

1. Compress the author preface / foreword.

2. Move the new-century and twin-life setup forward.

3. Strengthen the second digital correction accident.

4. Use Moon-Dark journey as the core experience after the accident.

Pass 3: Rewrite The Game Section

Make *Another World* the root of the trio's fate.

Focus:

  • First team login.
  • World selection.
  • Medieval hidden event.
  • Jim blocking the soldier.
  • Anna recording rules.
  • Charlie complaining but following.

Pass 4: Rebuild The Civilization History Section

Change the section from lecture to discovery chain.

Focus:

  • Less summary, more conflict.
  • Jared, Cross, and Shawn should each bring a different angle.
  • Jim should not sound like an encyclopedia. He should remain shaken and uncertain.

Pass 5: Rewrite The Moon Journey And Death

Make the ending feel inevitable.

Focus:

  • Jim's hesitation.
  • Dr. Lune's resistance and final companionship.
  • Spire's complicated position.
  • Anna and Charlie's sleeping bodies as emotional pressure.
  • Lunar death as destiny reached, not merely mission completed.

5. V2 Quality Test

After revising a chapter, check:

1. Is the chapter driven by character choice rather than author explanation?

2. Does it contain concrete scenes instead of pure exposition?

3. Does it clarify at least one of Jim, Anna, or Charlie?

4. Does it plant future-series seeds without overexplaining them?

5. Does it make the story feel naturally pulled toward the Moon rather than abruptly sent there?
