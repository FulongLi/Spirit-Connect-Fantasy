Voice Rules for The Dark Side of the Moon

Date: 2026-06-10

Voice Model

Chapter 1, "New Century", and Chapter 2, "The Second Correction", are the voice model for the rest of the V2 manuscript.

Later chapters should move toward that voice instead of becoming more report-like, omniscient, or grandly expository.

Core Voice

Jim is the primary narrator.

His voice should be:

  • Clear and direct.
  • Explanatory without becoming essay-like.
  • Lightly dry or humorous when Charlie is involved.
  • Grounded in what the event means to him before explaining what the event is.
  • Uncertain under pressure, especially around large cosmological truths.

Paragraph Rhythm

Prefer short paragraphs.

Important information should usually unfold in three beats:

  • fact
  • reaction
  • consequence

Do not pack too much setting explanation into a single long paragraph.

Sentence Patterns

The following patterns from the first two chapters are part of the manuscript's voice:

  • "This was not... at least not..."
  • "Not because... but because..."
  • "That was how I explained it at the time."
  • "Only later did I learn..."
  • "If... then...?"

These patterns preserve Jim's habit of defining events, then discovering that the definitions are inadequate.

Point of View

Even when a later chapter describes events Jim did not personally witness, the prose should enter through Jim's narrative frame:

  • "This was what Dr. Lune told me later."
  • "I was not there, but I watched the recording."
  • "I did not know this at the time."
  • "Only later did I understand..."

Avoid sudden omniscient narration unless the passage is clearly framed as a record, meeting transcript, or later reconstruction.

Handling Exposition

Worldbuilding must serve character choice.

When explaining a setting element, ask:

  • Does this make Jim more afraid or more certain?
  • How does it change Anna, Charlie, or someone else's situation?
  • How does it move the story toward the Moon's dark side?

If a passage only tells the reader that the world is large, compress it or turn it into a discovery inside a scene.

Dialogue

Preserve character differences:

  • Jim: asks, explains, takes responsibility.
  • Anna: confirms rules, defines boundaries, questions vague terms.
  • Charlie: jokes to hold fear down, but does not retreat at key moments.
  • Dr. Lune: controlled and cautious, caught between scientist and doctor.
  • Spire: calm and complicated, as if he knows more than he is willing to say.

Avoid

  • Do not turn Jim's uncertainty into settled conclusions.
  • Do not flatten character reactions into plot summary.
  • Do not let meeting chapters become full reports.
  • Do not remove small pauses, jokes, or hesitations that carry character.
  • Do not explain Elara, the Terasha Record, or Digital Jim too early.
